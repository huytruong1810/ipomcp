\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Compressed update and modeled solves}
\label{alg:filter}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{procedure} \textsc{Policy}$(\theta_j)$ \hfill ($\theta_j$ at level $k$)
\IF{$k = 0$}
    \STATE \textbf{return} the uniform distribution over $A_j$
\ENDIF
\IF{the canonical digest of $\theta_j$ is cached}
    \STATE \textbf{return} the cached policy
\ENDIF
\STATE run Algorithm~1 of the paper for $j$ at $b_j$ with the modeled budget, solving $j$'s models of $i$ by \textsc{Policy} at level $k-1$
\STATE \textbf{return} the uniform distribution over $\arg\max_{a_j} Q^\star$, and cache it
\STATE
\STATE \textbf{procedure} $\SEN_{\theta_i}(b_i, a_i, o_i)$
\STATE $b' \leftarrow 0$
\FORALL{$(s, \theta_j) \in \mathrm{supp}(b_i)$, $a_j$ with $\pi = \textsc{Policy}(\theta_j)(a_j) > 0$, $s'$, $o_j$}
    \STATE $w \leftarrow b_i(s, \theta_j)\, \pi\, T_i(s, a, s')\, O_i(s', a, o_i)\, O_j(s', a, o_j)$
    \STATE $\theta_j' \leftarrow \langle \SEN_{\theta_j}(b_j, a_j, o_j), \hth_j \rangle$ \hfill (unchanged at level 0)
    \STATE $b'(s', \theta_j') \leftarrow b'(s', \theta_j') + w$ \hfill (canonical merge)
\ENDFOR
\STATE normalize $b'$
\IF{$|\mathrm{supp}(b')| > N$}
    \STATE $b' \leftarrow$ $N$ stratified draws from $b'$ in canonical order, merged
\ENDIF
\STATE \textbf{return} $b'$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\paragraph{Adaptive modeled budget.} A modeled solve grows one search tree by doubling its number of simulations from a small pilot size. After each doubling it compares the gap between its two leading actions with a Bonferroni-corrected $t$ critical value times the spread of the same gap across a few independent pilot trees, and it stops as soon as the gap exceeds that threshold. At its cap, the solve is identical to a flat search with the cap's budget, so the rule is consistent.

\section{Guarantees: Soundness, Consistency and Compression}
\label{sec:theory}

This section states and proves the guarantees on which the paper's claims of verifiability rest: soundness of the certified intervals (T1), the belief-dependent bounds they use for what search leaves unknown, consistency (T2), a deterministic bound on the error of compressing $i$'s belief (T3), and soundness when the other agent's models are themselves solved by search (T4).

\paragraph{Assumptions.} Throughout, (A1) $S$, $A_i$, $A_j$, $\Omega_i$ and $\Omega_j$ are finite, and the nesting depth is finite; (A2) the horizon $H$ is finite and $\gamma \in [0, 1]$; (A3) the frames are known to $i$ (the frames are common knowledge, Section~2 of the paper); (A4) rewards are bounded, $R_{\min} \leq R_i \leq R_{\max}$, with declared bounds; (A5) every belief has finite support. An episode may end at any step, in which case the remaining return is zero.

\paragraph{The searched model.} Fix the configuration and the seeds. Every modeled solve is seeded from a digest of its model (Section~4.4), so each model $\theta_j$ that the planner queries receives a fixed action law $\pi_j(\cdot \mid \theta_j)$, and every belief that the planner forms is a fixed function of its parent, the action and the observation (the compressed update $\SEN$). Agent $i$'s problem with this action law and this update is a finite-horizon POMDP over the reachable interactive states, which are finitely many by (A1) and (A5). We call it the \emph{searched model}, write $Q^H_{\mathrm{s}}$ and $U^H_{\mathrm{s}}$ for its values, and write $\Pr(o, \mathrm{alive} \mid b, a)$ for the probability, under $b$, that $i$ observes $o$ and the episode continues. When every modeled level is solved exactly and no belief exceeds $N$ atoms, $\SEN = \SE$ and the searched model is the exact I-POMDP, so $Q^H_{\mathrm{s}} = Q^H$ of Eq.~2 of the paper. The guarantees below are therefore relative to $i$'s model of $j$: they certify a subjective best response, and they give no bound on exploitability by a $j$ outside that model.

\paragraph{Return bounds.} Let $\ell_0 = u_0 = 0$, $\ell_r = R_{\min} + \gamma \min(0, \ell_{r-1})$ and $u_r = R_{\max} + \gamma \max(0, u_{r-1})$, and $W_r = u_r - \ell_r$.

\paragraph{Notation.} An \emph{extended state} is $x=(s,m)$, where $m$ is a model of $j$ with action law
$\pi_j(\cdot\mid m)$ and successor $\tau(m,a_j,o_j)$. $S_\bot$ is the set of terminal physical states,
$\bar R(s,a_i,a_j)=\sum_{s'}T_i(s,\langle a_i,a_j\rangle,s')R_i(s,\langle a_i,a_j\rangle,s')$, and
$\kappa_{s,a_i,a_j}(o)=\sum_{s'\notin S_\bot}T_i(s,\langle a_i,a_j\rangle,s')\,O_i(s',\langle a_i,a_j\rangle,o)$.
For a belief $b$ over extended states, $b(s)=\sum_m b(s,m)$,
$w_b(s,a_j)=\sum_{m}b(s,m)\,\pi_j(a_j\mid m)$, and, for $s'\notin S_\bot$,
$\mu_{b,a,o}(s')=\sum_{s,a_j}w_b(s,a_j)\,T_i(s,\langle a,a_j\rangle,s')\,O_i(s',\langle a,a_j\rangle,o)$,
the unnormalized physical marginal of the posterior, so that
$\sum_{s'}\mu_{b,a,o}(s')=\Pr(o,\mathrm{alive}\mid b,a)$. $W_r=u_r-\ell_r$.
The \emph{Bayes searched model} is the searched model in which $i$'s own belief is not compressed
inside the lookahead; $Q^r_{\mathrm s}$, $U^r_{\mathrm s}$ are its values.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:range}
(i) For every update of $i$'s posterior, compressed or not, every belief $b$, action $a$ and $r \geq 1$, $\ell_r \leq Q^r_{\mathrm{s}}(b, a) \leq u_r$ and $\ell_r \leq U^r_{\mathrm{s}}(b) \leq u_r$; the same holds for rollout values. (ii) If $i$'s posterior is updated by Bayes' rule over a fixed finite set of interactive states, with the nested models advanced by fixed functions, then $Q^r(\cdot, a)$ is the maximum of at most $G_r$ linear functions $b \mapsto \alpha \cdot b$ with $\ell_r \leq \alpha(is) \leq u_r$, where $G_r$ is the number of conditional $(r-1)$-step plans of $i$ after $a$ (one per assignment of an $(r-1)$-step plan to each observation).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(i) By induction on $r$, from $Q^r_{\mathrm{s}}(b, a) = \ER_i(b, a) + \gamma \sum_o p_o\, U^{r-1}_{\mathrm{s}}(b_{ao})$ with $\ER_i(b, a) \in [R_{\min}, R_{\max}]$, $p_o \geq 0$ and $\sum_o p_o \in [0, 1]$: the continuation lies in $[\min(0, \ell_{r-1}), \max(0, u_{r-1})]$. A rollout's return obeys the same recursion along its sampled path. (ii) Under these conditions $i$'s problem is a finite POMDP over the interactive states, and the standard argument for POMDPs applies: a plan $\sigma$ has the value $\alpha_\sigma(is) = \ER_i(is, a) + \gamma \sum_{o} \Pr(o, \mathrm{alive} \mid is, a)\, \alpha_{\sigma(o)}(is, o)$, the value is the maximum over plans of $\alpha_\sigma \cdot b$, and the bounds on $\alpha_\sigma$ follow by the induction of (i).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{T1: Soundness.}

\begin{theorem}[Soundness of the certified intervals]
\label{thm:sound}
Assume (A1)--(A5). For every budget $n \geq |A_i|$, every seed, and every root action $a$, the scoring pass (Section~4.3 of the paper) returns $Q^-(\emptyset, a) \leq Q^H_{\mathrm{s}}(b_i, a) \leq Q^+(\emptyset, a)$. In particular, (i) if $Q^-(\emptyset, a^\star) \geq \max_{a \neq a^\star} Q^+(\emptyset, a)$, then $a^\star$ is optimal in the searched model; (ii) the first-action loss of any root action $a$ in the searched model is at most $\max_{a'} Q^+(\emptyset, a') - Q^-(\emptyset, a)$; and (iii) when every modeled level is solved exactly and no belief exceeds $N$ atoms, the intervals contain the exact I-POMDP values $Q^H(\langle b_i, \hth_i \rangle, a)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
We show by induction on the steps to go $r$ that the interval $I(h)$ computed at every history $h$ of the tree with $r$ steps to go contains $U^r_{\mathrm{s}}(b_h)$, and that the interval $I(h, a)$ of every action evaluated at $h$ contains $Q^r_{\mathrm{s}}(b_h, a)$. The scoring pass uses the same update $\SEN$ and the same modeled policies as the searched model, so $b_h$, $\ER_i(b_h, a)$ and $\Pr(o, \mathrm{alive} \mid b_h, a)$ are exact quantities of the searched model.
\begin{itemize}
\item $r = 1$: $I(h) = \{\max_a \ER_i(b_h, a)\} = \{U^1_{\mathrm{s}}(b_h)\}$.
\item A frontier with $r$ steps to go (a history reached once, valued by a rollout or by a bound): $I(h) = [\ell_r, u_r] \ni U^r_{\mathrm{s}}(b_h)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:range}(i), intersected with the bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier} computed from its own belief or, when its belief is not computed, from its parent's, which contain it by that theorem.
\item An evaluated action $a$ at $h$ with $r \geq 2$: $Q^r_{\mathrm{s}}(b_h, a) = \ER_i(b_h, a) + \gamma \sum_o p_o\, U^{r-1}_{\mathrm{s}}(b_{hao})$ with $p_o = \Pr(o, \mathrm{alive} \mid b_h, a) \geq 0$. Each branch enters as one of the following: with $r = 2$, its exact value $U^1_{\mathrm{s}}(b_{hao})$, sampled or not; when sampled with $r \geq 3$, $p_o\, I(hao)$, which contains $p_o U^{r-1}_{\mathrm{s}}(b_{hao})$ by the induction hypothesis; when unsampled with $r \geq 3$, $p_o [\ell_{r-1}, u_{r-1}]$, which contains it by Lemma~\ref{lem:range}, intersected with the branch bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier}, which contain it too. A sum of intervals contains the sum of their members, and $\gamma \geq 0$.
\item An action never tried at $h$, or ruled out before it was tried: since $\sum_o p_o \in [0, 1]$, $Q^r_{\mathrm{s}}(b_h, a) \in \ER_i(b_h, a) + \gamma [\min(0, \ell_{r-1}), \max(0, u_{r-1})]$, intersected with $[L^r(b_h, a), Q^r_{\mathrm{I\text{-}FIB}}(b_h, a)]$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier}.
\item An expanded history: $U^r_{\mathrm{s}}(b_h) = \max_a Q^r_{\mathrm{s}}(b_h, a)$ over all legal actions, tried or not. If $x_a \in [\lambda_a, \mu_a]$ for every $a$, then $\max_a \lambda_a \leq \max_a x_a \leq \max_a \mu_a$, and $I(h) = [\max_a \lambda_a, \max_a \mu_a]$.
\end{itemize}
Every root action receives an interval, by the third case if it was evaluated and by the fourth otherwise. Certified search also keeps, at every expanded history, bounds on each action computed by the same rules from deeper histories; each interval is intersected with them, and an intersection of intervals that contain a value contains it. (Ranges of values under any update are by Lemma~\ref{lem:range}(i).) Hence $Q^H_{\mathrm{s}}(b_i, a) \in I(\emptyset, a)$. Statement (i) follows because $Q^H_{\mathrm{s}}(b_i, a^\star) \geq Q^-(\emptyset, a^\star) \geq Q^+(\emptyset, a) \geq Q^H_{\mathrm{s}}(b_i, a)$ for every $a \neq a^\star$. Statement (ii) follows from $U^H_{\mathrm{s}}(b_i) \leq \max_{a'} Q^+(\emptyset, a')$ and $Q^H_{\mathrm{s}}(b_i, a) \geq Q^-(\emptyset, a)$. Statement (iii) follows because the searched model is then the exact I-POMDP.
\end{proof}

The statement holds in exact arithmetic. The implementation sums with compensated summation, and the experiments test containment to $10^{-9}$.

\paragraph{Belief-dependent frontier bounds.} The scalar range $[\ell_r, u_r]$ ignores the belief and the end of the episode. In UAV reconnaissance no return exceeds the reward for spotting the target, 50, yet $u_3 = 142.6$. The bounds below are computed from the belief at hand.

\begin{definition}[Partner-optimistic and partner-pessimistic vectors]
For $s\notin S_\bot$ and $r\ge1$, with $\bar Q^0\equiv0$ and $\underline\alpha^0_a\equiv0$,
\begin{align*}
\bar F^r(s,a_i,a_j)&=\bar R(s,a_i,a_j)+\gamma\sum_{o}\max_{a'}\sum_{s'\notin S_\bot}
T_i(s,\langle a_i,a_j\rangle,s')\,O_i(s',\langle a_i,a_j\rangle,o)\,\bar Q^{r-1}(s',a'),\\
\bar Q^r(s,a_i)&=\max_{a_j}\bar F^r(s,a_i,a_j),\\
\underline\alpha^r_{a}(s)&=\min_{a_j}\Big[\bar R(s,a,a_j)+\gamma\sum_{s'\notin S_\bot}
T_i(s,\langle a,a_j\rangle,s')\,\underline\alpha^{r-1}_{a}(s')\Big].
\end{align*}
Let $\mathcal B_1=\{\beta_{s,a_i,a_j,o}\}$ be the physical one-step beliefs,
$\beta_{s,a_i,a_j,o}(s')=T_i(s,\langle a_i,a_j\rangle,s')O_i(s',\langle a_i,a_j\rangle,o)/\kappa_{s,a_i,a_j}(o)$,
and define on $\mathcal B_1$, with $\bar Q^0_{\mathrm T}\equiv0$,
\begin{multline*}
\bar Q^r_{\mathrm T}(\beta,a')=\min\Big\{\sum_{s'}\beta(s')\bar Q^r(s',a'),\;
\sum_{s'}\beta(s')\max_{a_j'}\bar R(s',a',a_j')\\
+\gamma\sum_{o'}\max_{a''}\sum_{s'}\beta(s')\max_{a_j'}
\kappa_{s',a',a_j'}(o')\,\bar Q^{r-1}_{\mathrm T}(\beta_{s',a',a_j',o'},a'')\Big\}.
\end{multline*}
For a belief $b$ over extended states,
\begin{align*}
Q^r_{\mathrm{I\text{-}FIB}}(b,a)&=\ER_i(b,a)+\gamma\sum_o\max_{a'}\sum_{s'}\mu_{b,a,o}(s')\,\bar Q^{r-1}(s',a'),\\
Q^r_{\mathrm{I\text{-}TIB}}(b,a)&=\ER_i(b,a)+\gamma\sum_o\max_{a'}\sum_{s,a_j}w_b(s,a_j)\,
\kappa_{s,a,a_j}(o)\,\bar Q^{r-1}_{\mathrm T}(\beta_{s,a,a_j,o},a'),\\
L^r(b,a)&=\ER_i(b,a)+\gamma\sum_o\max_{a'}\sum_{s'}\mu_{b,a,o}(s')\,\underline\alpha^{r-1}_{a'}(s').
\end{align*}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Frontier soundness]\label{thm:frontier}
Assume (A1)--(A5). In the Bayes searched model, for every belief $b$, action $a$ and $r\ge1$,
\[
\ell_r\;\le\;L^r(b,a)\;\le\;Q^r_{\mathrm s}(b,a)\;\le\;Q^r_{\mathrm{I\text{-}TIB}}(b,a)\;\le\;
Q^r_{\mathrm{I\text{-}FIB}}(b,a)\;\le\;\sum_s b(s)\,\bar Q^r(s,a)\;\le\;u_r .
\]
Moreover, for every action $a$ and observation $o$ with $p_o=\Pr(o,\mathrm{alive}\mid b,a)>0$,
\[
\max_{a'}\sum_{s'}\mu_{b,a,o}(s')\,\underline\alpha^{r}_{a'}(s')\;\le\;p_o\,U^{r}_{\mathrm s}(b_{ao})\;\le\;
\max_{a'}\sum_{s'}\mu_{b,a,o}(s')\,\bar Q^{r}(s',a'),
\]
so an observation branch is bounded from its parent's belief, without computing $b_{ao}$.
If $T_i$ is deterministic, $Q^r_{\mathrm{I\text{-}TIB}}=Q^r_{\mathrm{I\text{-}FIB}}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Unit bound.} We show $Q^r_{\mathrm s}(b,a)\le\sum_s b(s)\bar Q^r(s,a)$ for all $b,a$ by induction on $r$.
For $r=1$, $Q^1_{\mathrm s}(b,a)=\sum_{s,a_j}w_b(s,a_j)\bar R(s,a,a_j)\le\sum_s b(s)\max_{a_j}\bar R(s,a,a_j)$,
since $\sum_{a_j}w_b(s,a_j)=b(s)$. For $r\ge2$, Bayes' rule gives $p_o\,b_{ao}(s')=\mu_{b,a,o}(s')$, so by the
induction hypothesis at $b_{ao}$,
\[
Q^r_{\mathrm s}(b,a)=\ER_i(b,a)+\gamma\sum_o\max_{a'}p_o\,Q^{r-1}_{\mathrm s}(b_{ao},a')
\le\ER_i(b,a)+\gamma\sum_o\max_{a'}\sum_{s'}\mu_{b,a,o}(s')\bar Q^{r-1}(s',a')=Q^r_{\mathrm{I\text{-}FIB}}(b,a).
\]
Writing $\mu_{b,a,o}$ and $\ER_i$ as sums over $(s,a_j)$ weighted by $w_b$ and moving $\max_{a'}$ inside the sum,
$Q^r_{\mathrm{I\text{-}FIB}}(b,a)\le\sum_{s,a_j}w_b(s,a_j)\bar F^r(s,a,a_j)\le\sum_s b(s)\bar Q^r(s,a)$.
This proves the unit bound and the fifth inequality. The sixth follows by induction from
$\bar R\le R_{\max}$ and $\sum_o\sum_{s'\notin S_\bot}T_iO_i\le1$, which bound the continuation by $\max(0,u_{r-1})$.

\emph{One-step beliefs.} We show that every extended belief $b'$ whose physical marginal is some
$\beta\in\mathcal B_1$ satisfies $Q^r_{\mathrm s}(b',a')\le\bar Q^r_{\mathrm T}(\beta,a')$, by induction on $r$.
The first argument of the minimum is the unit bound. For the second,
$\ER_i(b',a')\le\sum_{s'}\beta(s')\max_{a_j'}\bar R(s',a',a_j')$. The posterior $b'_{a'o'}$ is the convex
combination, with weights $b'(x')\pi_j(a_j'\mid m')\kappa_{s',a',a_j'}(o')/p_{o'}$, of the extended one-step
beliefs obtained from a known $x'=(s',m')$ and a known $a_j'$, each of which has physical marginal
$\beta_{s',a',a_j',o'}\in\mathcal B_1$. Since $Q^{r-1}_{\mathrm s}(\cdot,a'')$ is convex
(Lemma~\ref{lem:range}(ii)), the induction hypothesis gives
$p_{o'}Q^{r-1}_{\mathrm s}(b'_{a'o'},a'')\le\sum_{x',a_j'}b'(x')\pi_j(a_j'\mid m')\kappa_{s',a',a_j'}(o')
\bar Q^{r-1}_{\mathrm T}(\beta_{s',a',a_j',o'},a'')$, and an average over $a_j'$ and $m'$ is at most the
maximum over $a_j'$.

\emph{Third and fourth inequalities.} Decompose $b_{ao}$ in the same way, into extended one-step beliefs with
weights $b(x)\pi_j(a_j\mid m)\kappa_{s,a,a_j}(o)/p_o$; convexity and the previous paragraph give
$Q^r_{\mathrm s}\le Q^r_{\mathrm{I\text{-}TIB}}$. Replacing $\bar Q^{r-1}_{\mathrm T}(\beta,a')$ by the first
argument of its minimum and using $\kappa_{s,a,a_j}(o)\beta_{s,a,a_j,o}(s')=T_iO_i$ gives
$Q^r_{\mathrm{I\text{-}TIB}}\le Q^r_{\mathrm{I\text{-}FIB}}$.

\emph{Lower bounds.} Let $V^r_a(x)$ be the value, from $x=(s,m)$, of repeating action $a$ for $r$ steps.
$V^r_a(x)=\sum_{a_j}\pi_j(a_j\mid m)[\bar R(s,a,a_j)+\gamma\sum_{s',o_j}T_iO_jV^{r-1}_a(s',\tau(m,a_j,o_j))]
\ge\underline\alpha^r_a(s)$ by induction, since an average over $a_j$ is at least the minimum. Hence
$U^{r-1}_{\mathrm s}(b_{ao})\ge\max_{a'}\sum_{s'}b_{ao}(s')\underline\alpha^{r-1}_{a'}(s')$, and substituting in
Eq.~2 of the paper gives $L^r\le Q^r_{\mathrm s}$. $\underline\alpha^r_a\ge\ell_r$ by the induction of
Lemma~\ref{lem:range}(i), which gives $\ell_r\le L^r$. The displayed branch bounds are the unit bound and the
blind bound at $b_{ao}$, multiplied by $p_o$. If $T_i$ is deterministic every $\beta\in\mathcal B_1$ is a point
mass, the second argument of the minimum decouples $a_j'$ across terms and so is at least the first, and
$\bar Q^r_{\mathrm T}(\delta_{s'},\cdot)=\bar Q^r(s',\cdot)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
(i) Krale et al.\ define FIB and TIB for one infinite-horizon POMDP. Here the recursion is over steps to go, the
state is the physical state, and the other agent's later actions are maximized, which is what keeps the tables
of size $|S||A_i||A_j|$ instead of the size of the reachable model space. Their optimized and entropy variants
need a linear program per $(b,a,o)$ and are not used. (ii) If $i$'s belief is compressed inside the lookahead,
the bounds remain sound when evaluated at a node's own belief and widened by the compression slack of
Theorem~\ref{thm:compress-det}; branch bounds from the parent's belief are then sound for the model that does
not compress below the parent.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{T2: Consistency.}

\begin{theorem}[Consistency]
\label{thm:consistent}
Assume (A1)--(A5), $R_{\max} > R_{\min}$, $\gamma > 0$ and $c > 0$, and treat the planner's random stream as independent uniform draws. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] the probability that a search of $n$ simulations ends with a tree that satisfies the condition of Proposition~1 of the paper (every action evaluated at every history with at least two steps to go, and every positive branch present at every history with at least three) tends to 1 as $n \to \infty$; on that event, $Q^\star(\emptyset, \cdot) = Q^H_{\mathrm{s}}(b_i, \cdot)$, the certified intervals have zero width, and the decision is optimal in the searched model;
\item[(ii)] if, in addition, every modeled level is solved by I-POMCP with flat budgets that also tend to infinity, and no belief exceeds $N$ atoms (for example, exact beliefs), then the probability that the root decision is an optimal action of the exact I-POMDP tends to 1.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) The tree has depth $H - 1$ and finite branching, so it has finitely many possible histories. Call a history \emph{recurrent} if it is visited infinitely often in an infinite run of simulations of a single tree. The root is recurrent.

\emph{Every action at a recurrent history is selected infinitely often.} Untried actions are selected first. Suppose action $a$ is selected only $K < \infty$ times at a recurrent history $h$. Values are bounded (Lemma~\ref{lem:range}, which also covers rollout values), say within $[\ell, u]$, so the index of $a$ in Eq.~3 of the paper is at least $\ell + c W_r \sqrt{\log N(h) / K}$. An action $a'$ selected at a visit with count $N(h, a') = m$ therefore satisfies $u + c W_r \sqrt{\log N(h)/m} \geq \ell + c W_r \sqrt{\log N(h)/K}$, which forces $m \leq K (1 + o(1))$ as $N(h) \to \infty$. Then the counts of the finitely many actions stay bounded while their sum $N(h)$ grows without bound, a contradiction.

\emph{Every positive branch of a recurrent pair is created, and is recurrent.} Each visit to $(h, a)$ draws an interactive state from $b_h$ and then $j$'s action, the next state and $o$, so the observation is drawn from $\Pr(\cdot, \mathrm{alive} \mid b_h, a)$, independently across visits. A branch $o$ of probability $p > 0$ is missed in $k$ visits with probability $(1 - p)^k \to 0$; once created, it is visited on a fraction $p$ of the visits to $(h, a)$ and is therefore recurrent. A child reached once is valued by a rollout and is expanded at its next visit.

By induction on depth, every history with at least two steps to go that is reachable with positive probability is recurrent, so every one of its actions is evaluated, and every positive branch of a history with at least three steps to go is present. Since the reachable tree is finite, the probability that all of them are present after $n$ simulations tends to 1. On that event the condition of Proposition~1 holds, every interval is a point (Theorem~\ref{thm:sound}, with no frontier, fallback or untried action left), and the greedy decision maximizes $Q^H_{\mathrm{s}}(b_i, \cdot)$, up to the tie tolerance. The seed of a search of $n$ simulations depends on $n$, so the statement is in probability, not along a single stream; along a single stream of one tree grown through increasing budgets, the same argument gives almost-sure exactness after finitely many simulations.

(ii) With finite supports and finite nesting, the set of modeled beliefs that the root search can query is finite. By (i) applied to each modeled solve, by induction on the level, and by a union bound over this finite set, the probability that every modeled solve is complete tends to 1. On that event every modeled agent plays uniformly over its exact maximizers, with the same tie tolerance as the exact reference, so its action law equals the exact I-POMDP's, and the searched model is the exact I-POMDP. A second application of (i) at the root concludes the proof.
\end{proof}

The theorem says more than asymptotic convergence: once the tree is complete, the scored values are exact, not merely close. This is the role of exact scoring with two steps to go. Without it, the leaves of the last level would carry sampled frequencies, and the scored values would converge only in the limit. Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} makes the property checkable at every finite budget, since a width of zero certifies completeness.

\paragraph{T3: Compression.} A bound on compression from Hoeffding's inequality with a union over $i$'s plans is valid but vacuous at the sizes used ($N = 64$ gives about 74 reward units in UAV at $H = 3$, against decision gaps near 1). The union is an artifact of bounding a random maximum: a deterministic $L_1$ bound holds for all plans at once.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:l1}
Let $f(b)=\max_\sigma\alpha_\sigma\cdot b$ with $\underline c\le\alpha_\sigma(x)\le\bar c$ for all $\sigma,x$.
Then $|f(b)-f(b')|\le\frac12(\bar c-\underline c)\,\|b-b'\|_1$ for all beliefs $b,b'$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For one $\alpha$ and $c=(\bar c+\underline c)/2$, $\alpha\cdot(b-b')=(\alpha-c)\cdot(b-b')$ because both beliefs
sum to one, and $|\alpha-c|\le(\bar c-\underline c)/2$. A maximum of functions with a common modulus has it.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Head--tail compression]
Given $N$ and $0\le n_t<N$, a belief with more than $N$ atoms keeps its $N-n_t$ heaviest atoms (ties in
canonical order) with their exact masses, and replaces the remaining atoms, of total mass $\varepsilon$, by
$n_t$ stratified draws from them in canonical order, each of mass $\varepsilon/n_t$. With $n_t=0$ the kept
masses are divided by $1-\varepsilon$. $\varepsilon$ is recorded as the \emph{loss} of the compression.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Deterministic compression bound]\label{thm:compress-det}
(a) Head--tail compression satisfies $\|\hat b-b\|_1\le2\varepsilon$ for every draw, and
$\mathbb E[\hat b]=b$ when $n_t\ge1$. With $n_t=0$, $\|\hat b-b\|_1=2\varepsilon$, the minimum of
$\|b'-b\|_1$ over all beliefs $b'$ with at most $N$ atoms.
(b) Let $\widetilde Q^r$ be the values under the update that compresses $i$'s own posterior by head--tail
compression whenever its support exceeds $N$, and $Q^r$ the values under the same update without compressing
$i$'s posterior, with the nested models held as in the searched model. Let $\varepsilon_h$ be the loss
recorded at history $h$ of the tree (zero if its belief was not compressed), $c_1\equiv0$ and
\[
c_r(h,a)=\gamma\sum_o\Pr(o,\mathrm{alive}\mid b_h,a)\big[\bar W_{r-1}\,\varepsilon_{hao}+\max_{a'}c_{r-1}(hao,a')\big],
\]
where a branch whose value is computed from its own belief (an expanded child, and every branch with two steps
to go, which scoring values exactly) is charged as written, and any other branch contributes zero, its value
being bounded from its parent's belief (Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier}). Then, for every draw and every root action,
$|\widetilde Q^H(b_i,a)-Q^H(b_i,a)|\le c_H(\emptyset,a)\le\sum_{k=1}^{H-1}\gamma^k\bar W_{H-k}\,
\bar\varepsilon_k$, where $\bar\varepsilon_k$ is the largest loss at depth $k$ and
$\bar W_r=\max_{s,a}\bar Q^r(s,a)-\min_s\underline w^r(s)\le W_r$, with
$\underline w^r(s)=\min_{a,a_j}[\bar R(s,a,a_j)+\gamma\sum_{s'\notin S_\bot}T_i\,\underline w^{r-1}(s')]$.
Consequently $[Q^-(\emptyset,a)-c_H(\emptyset,a),\,Q^+(\emptyset,a)+c_H(\emptyset,a)]$ contains the value of
the searched model that does not compress $i$'s belief. The planner computes $c_H$ with $W_r \geq \bar W_r$ in place of $\bar W_r$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a) The kept atoms are exact, so $\hat b-b$ is supported on the tail and its replacement, each of mass
$\varepsilon$; stratified draws are unbiased. For $n_t=0$, any $b'$ supported on a set $A$ of at most $N$
atoms has $\|b'-b\|_1\ge b(A^c)+|b'(A)-b(A)|=2b(A^c)\ge2\varepsilon$, with equality for the renormalized head.
(b) Every plan's vector lies between $\underline w^r(s)$ and $\max_a\bar Q^r(s,a)$ (the unit bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:frontier} at a point belief, and the same induction with a minimum), so by
Lemma~\ref{lem:l1}, $|U^{r}(\hat b)-U^{r}(b)|\le\bar W_r\varepsilon$. The evidence is computed exactly under the
current (possibly compressed) belief, so both updates weight the branches by the same probabilities, and
$|\widetilde Q^r(b,a)-Q^r(b,a)|\le\gamma\sum_op_o\big(|\widetilde U^{r-1}(\hat b_o)-U^{r-1}(\hat b_o)|
+|U^{r-1}(\hat b_o)-U^{r-1}(b_o)|\big)$, which is the recursion. No probability and no union bound is involved.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The bound concerns compression of $i$'s own belief below the root. Compression of a nested belief enters through $j$'s predicted action, which can switch discontinuously near a tie, so no bound of this form holds for it; the planner's answer is T4 below, under which a tie that cannot be certified broken stays a tie. A near-uniform belief over many more than $N$ atoms has a loss near 1, and the bound is then void.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{T4: Searched models of the other agent.}

\paragraph{Setting.} Index the models of $j$ by \emph{model nodes} $m=(\theta_j^0,h_j)$: an initial model in the
support of the root belief and a private history of $j$. In the exact I-POMDP, $m$ carries the exact belief
$b_j^\ast(m)$, the exact values $Q_j^\ast(m,\cdot)$ and the law
$\pi^\ast(\cdot\mid m)=\mathrm{Unif}(\OPT_\tau(m))$, where $\OPT_\tau(m)=\{a_j:Q_j^\ast(m,a_j)\ge
\max Q_j^\ast(m,\cdot)-\tau\}$ and $\tau\ge0$ is the tie tolerance of the exact solution. The planner holds a
belief $\tilde b_j(m)$ and a law $\tilde\pi(\cdot\mid m)$. Both processes move on extended states
$x=(s,m)$ with the same kernels, and differ only in the law at each node. A node is \emph{grounded} if
$\tilde b_j(m)=b_j^\ast(m)$. Suppose that at a grounded node the nested solve returns intervals with
$Q_j^-(m,a_j)\le Q_j^\ast(m,a_j)\le Q_j^+(m,a_j)$, and let $M^-=\max_{a_j}Q_j^-$, $M^+=\max_{a_j}Q_j^+$,
\[
\widehat{\OPT}(m)=\{a_j:Q_j^+(m,a_j)\ge M^--\tau\},\qquad
\underline{\OPT}(m)=\{a_j:Q_j^-(m,a_j)\ge M^+-\tau\}.
\]
The node is \emph{resolved} if $\underline{\OPT}(m)=\widehat{\OPT}(m)$. The planner predicts
$\tilde\pi(\cdot\mid m)=\mathrm{Unif}(\widehat{\OPT}(m))$, and
$\hat\delta(m)=1-\max(1,|\underline{\OPT}(m)|)/|\widehat{\OPT}(m)|$; at a node that is not grounded,
$\hat\delta(m)=1$. For a belief $b$ let $d(b)=\sum_{(s,m)}b(s,m)\hat\delta(m)$.

\begin{theorem}[Bi-level soundness]\label{thm:bilevel}
Assume (A1)--(A5) and no compression of beliefs.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] $\underline{\OPT}(m)\subseteq\OPT_\tau(m)\subseteq\widehat{\OPT}(m)$ at every grounded node, and
$\|\pi^\ast(\cdot\mid m)-\tilde\pi(\cdot\mid m)\|_{TV}\le\hat\delta(m)$ at every node.
\item[(ii)] If $m$ is resolved, $\tilde\pi(\cdot\mid m)=\pi^\ast(\cdot\mid m)$. If every node used in forming
$\tilde b_j(m)$ is resolved, $m$ is grounded.
\item[(iii)] Let $[Q^-(\emptyset,a),Q^+(\emptyset,a)]$ be the intervals of Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} for the
searched model with law $\tilde\pi$, let $e^0\equiv0$, and for a history $h$ of the tree with $r$ steps to go let
\[
e^r(h)=\min\Big\{W_r,\;W_r\,d(b_h)+\gamma\max_{a}\sum_{o}\Pr(o,\mathrm{alive}\mid b_h,a)\,e^{r-1}(hao)\Big\},
\]
with $e^r(h)=W_r$ at a history that the tree has not expanded. Then, for every root action $a$, the exact
I-POMDP value satisfies
\[
Q^-(\emptyset,a)-\Delta(a)\;\le\;Q^H(\langle b_i,\hth_i\rangle,a)\;\le\;Q^+(\emptyset,a)+\Delta(a),\quad
\Delta(a)=\min\Big\{W_H,\;W_H\,d(b_i)+\gamma\sum_o\Pr(o,\mathrm{alive}\mid b_i,a)\,e^{H-1}(ao)\Big\}.
\]
\item[(iv)] The widened intervals of (iii), computed by the solver of level $l-1$, satisfy the hypothesis on
nested intervals at level $l$. Hence, by induction on the level from level 1, whose models of level 0 are exact,
the guarantee holds at every level, for every budget and seed. If every model node that the planner can reach
within the lookahead is resolved, then $\Delta\equiv0$, the searched model is the exact I-POMDP, and the
intervals of Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} contain the exact values.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) If $a_j\in\OPT_\tau(m)$ then $Q_j^+(a_j)\ge Q_j^\ast(a_j)\ge\max Q_j^\ast-\tau\ge M^--\tau$. If
$a_j\in\underline{\OPT}(m)$ then $Q_j^\ast(a_j)\ge Q_j^-(a_j)\ge M^+-\tau\ge\max Q_j^\ast-\tau$. So
$\pi^\ast=\mathrm{Unif}(B)$ with $\underline{\OPT}\subseteq B\subseteq\widehat{\OPT}$ and $B\neq\emptyset$, and
$\|\mathrm{Unif}(B)-\mathrm{Unif}(\widehat{\OPT})\|_{TV}=1-|B|/|\widehat{\OPT}|\le\hat\delta(m)$. Total
variation never exceeds 1, which covers nodes that are not grounded. (ii) is immediate from (i) and from the
fact that the exact update of $j$'s belief is a function of the laws at the nodes below it.

(iii) Fix a conditional plan $\sigma$ of $i$ with $r$ steps to go and write $V^\ast(x;\sigma)$ and
$\tilde V(x;\sigma)$ for its values from $x=(s,m)$ under $\pi^\ast$ and $\tilde\pi$. With
$G^\ast(x,a_j)=\bar R+\gamma\sum_{x',o}P(x',o\mid x,\sigma_0,a_j)V^\ast(x';\sigma(o))$ and $\tilde G$ defined
alike,
\[
V^\ast(x;\sigma)-\tilde V(x;\sigma)=\sum_{a_j}\big(\pi^\ast-\tilde\pi\big)(a_j\mid m)\,G^\ast(x,a_j)
+\sum_{a_j}\tilde\pi(a_j\mid m)\big(G^\ast-\tilde G\big)(x,a_j).
\]
$G^\ast(x,\cdot)\in[\ell_r,u_r]$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:range}(i), so the first sum is at most
$\hat\delta(m)W_r$ in absolute value. The second is $\gamma$ times the expectation, under the planner's law, of
$V^\ast-\tilde V$ at the successor. Hence, for $\epsilon^r(b;\sigma)=\sum_xb(x)|V^\ast(x;\sigma)-\tilde
V(x;\sigma)|$,
\[
\epsilon^r(b;\sigma)\le W_r\,d(b)+\gamma\sum_o\Pr(o,\mathrm{alive}\mid b,\sigma_0)\,
\epsilon^{r-1}(\tilde b_{\sigma_0o};\sigma(o)),
\]
because the planner's posterior $\tilde b_{\sigma_0o}$ is the normalized successor measure under
$\tilde\pi$. Also $\epsilon^r\le W_r$, since both values lie in $[\ell_r,u_r]$. By induction,
$\sup_\sigma\epsilon^r(b_h;\sigma)\le e^r(h)$ at every history of the tree. Finally
$|\max_\sigma V^\ast(b;\sigma)-\max_\sigma\tilde V(b;\sigma)|\le\sup_\sigma\epsilon(b;\sigma)$, taken over the
plans that start with $a$, gives $|Q^H-\tilde Q^H|(b_i,a)\le\Delta(a)$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} bounds
$\tilde Q^H$. The recursion follows only the planner's own transitions; branches that only the exact law
reaches are covered by the range of $G^\ast$.

(iv) The intervals of (iii) at level $l-1$ contain $Q_j^\ast$ at every grounded node, which is the hypothesis.
At level 1 the modeled agents are level 0, whose law is known, so $\hat\delta\equiv0$ and (iii) is
Theorem~\ref{thm:sound}(iii). If every reachable node is resolved, every node is grounded by (ii) and
$d\equiv0$ on the tree; the rule that unexpanded histories carry $W_r$ is then not needed, because no node
below them can deviate.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Deterministic stopping.} A modeled solve grows one tree by doubling to its cap, computes the certified root intervals after each doubling, widened by $\Delta$, and stops when the root is resolved: every action satisfies $Q^+ < M^- - \tau$ or $Q^- \geq M^+ - \tau$. There are no pilot trees and no distributional assumption. The plausible set contains the exact optimal set at every checkpoint (Theorem~\ref{thm:bilevel}(i)); on a tree whose non-dominated part is complete, the intervals of the surviving actions are points, so the rule stops; at the cap the solve returns the plausible set flagged unresolved, and its uncertainty is charged to the caller through $\hat\delta$. The relative tolerance of the exact solution is made absolute conservatively: $\tau^+ = \tau \max(1, |M^-|, |M^+|)$ in the plausible set and $\tau^- = \tau \max(1, \min(|M^-|, |M^+|))$ in the certain set ($\tau$ when $[M^-, M^+]$ contains 0).

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:consistent} is stated for the index of Eq.~3 of the paper. The certified planner also ranks actions by their upper bounds and stops when the root is resolved; Theorems~\ref{thm:sound}, \ref{thm:frontier}, \ref{thm:compress-det} and \ref{thm:bilevel} hold for it at every budget, whatever the selection rule, and a width of zero still certifies an exact value.
\end{remark}

\section{Coverage of the Certified Intervals}
\label{sec:coverage}

Theorem~\ref{thm:sound} is a statement about every budget, so we test it at every budget. On the stored reachable beliefs of Section~7.1 of the paper, each with its exact I-POMDP action values, we grow one search tree per decision to 4{,}000 simulations and score it at 10, 30, 100, 300, 1{,}000 and 4{,}000. The tree is seeded as the 4{,}000-simulation search, so each smaller stage is a prefix of it. We use two configurations. With \emph{exact modeled levels} and exact beliefs, the searched model is the exact I-POMDP (Theorem~\ref{thm:sound}(iii)), so every interval must contain the exact value. \paragraph{Soundness.} With exact modeled levels, the intervals contained the exact values of every action in every decision at every budget: 744 decisions, up to six budgets each, no exception. The certified loss bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:sound}(ii) was never below the actual loss.

\paragraph{Informativeness.} Where the tree can be completed, the intervals decide. At UAV $H = 3$, every one of the 512 decisions is certified optimal by separation (Theorem~\ref{thm:sound}(i)) at 1{,}000 simulations, at a median of 21~s per decision against 48~s for the exact solver. In coordination tiger at level 3, all 28 decisions are certified optimal at 100 simulations and exact (width zero) at 1{,}000, at a median of 1{,}255~s against 1{,}789~s; there, nearly all of the cost is the exact solution of the modeled levels. At $H = 4$ and 5 they are sound but loose: the median width at 4{,}000 simulations is 74 and 100 reward units, because every unexpanded frontier is charged the full range of returns, and separation certifies 28 of 120 decisions at $H = 4$ and none at $H = 5$. A tighter frontier bound, for example from a heuristic value with its own certificate, would narrow them; we leave this to future work.

\paragraph{What the certificate does not see.} With the own budget, the intervals shrink around the values of the searched model. At UAV $H = 3$, 1{,}000 simulations give width zero on 266 of 512 decisions, yet only 13 intervals contain the exact values; the gap between the searched and exact values has median 0.97 and maximum 3.7 reward units. At $H = 4$, coverage falls from 113 of 120 at 10 simulations to none at 1{,}000, and the share of optimal decisions falls from 99 of 120 at 100 simulations to 46: deeper search converges on the best response to a modeled target that is more predictable than the exact one. The certificate cannot detect this error, by design; only the comparison with the exact solver does. This is why every result in the paper is reported beside the exact solution, and why the guarantee is stated as a subjective best response.

\section{The Conditional Value of Depth}
\label{sec:depth-theory}

This section proves Theorem~2 of the paper, which states what one more level guarantees when $i$ does not know $j$'s level, gives two further corollaries, and describes the exact check of Section~7.3 of the paper.

\paragraph{Setting.} Fix the stepping agent $i$, the lookahead $H$ and uniform nesting: the agent at level $l$ holds a uniform prior over $j$'s levels $0, \ldots, l-1$, and so does every modeled agent. Call $\{0, \ldots, l-1\}$ the \emph{support} of level $l$. For a history $h$ of $i$ and a true level $m$ of $j$, let $b^m(h)$ be the exact conditional distribution of the physical state and $j$'s model given $h$ and that $j$ is at level $m$. For a conditional $H$-step plan $\sigma$ of $i$ from $h$ (an action now, and one for each later history within the lookahead), let $V_m(h, \sigma) = \alpha_\sigma \cdot b^m(h)$ be its true expected $H$-step reward against level $m$, with $\alpha_\sigma$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:range}(ii). The \emph{receding plan} $\sigma_l(h)$ of the exact agent at level $l$ takes, with $r$ steps to go, a maximizer of its own $Q^r$ (uniformly over ties).

\begin{theorem}[Bayes dominance on the support]
\label{thm:dominance}
Assume (A1)--(A5), and that the agent at level $k+1$ is exact: its beliefs are updated exactly, and its model of each level $m' \leq k$ of $j$ has the frame, the nested prior and the exact policy of the real agent at that level. Then for every history $h$ that it reaches with positive probability and every plan $\sigma$, including the receding plan of level $k$,
\[
\sum_{m' = 0}^{k} \Pr_{k+1}(m' \mid h)\, \big[V_{m'}(h, \sigma_{k+1}) - V_{m'}(h, \sigma)\big] \;\geq\; 0,
\]
with equality if and only if $\sigma$ is also optimal for level $k+1$'s belief at $h$. Consequently:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(Confidence.)} If $\Pr_{k+1}(m \mid h) = 1 - \epsilon > 0$ for the true level $m \leq k$, then $V_m(h, \sigma_{k+1}) - V_m(h, \sigma) \geq -\frac{\epsilon}{1 - \epsilon}\,(u_H - \ell_H)$. With a correct point prior ($\epsilon = 0$) one more level never loses at a decision.
\item[(ii)] \emph{(Hedging.)} A loss against one level of the support is repaid, weighted by the posterior, by gains against the others: if the bracket is negative for $m$, the posterior-weighted sum of the brackets over $m' \neq m$ is at least $\Pr_{k+1}(m \mid h)$ times its magnitude.
\item[(iii)] \emph{(On average.)} For any fixed policy of $i$ that generates the histories, the prior-weighted expected gain at every step, $\sum_{m \leq k} \mu(m)\, \mathbb{E}_m[V_m(h, \sigma_{k+1}) - V_m(h, \sigma_k)]$, is nonnegative.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Level $k+1$'s belief at $h$ is $b_{k+1}(h) = \sum_{m'} \Pr_{k+1}(m' \mid h)\, b^{m'}(h)$: its prior is the mixture of its models, it conditions on $h$ exactly, and each of its models equals the real agent at that level, so the component of level $m'$ is the exact conditional $b^{m'}(h)$. Every plan's value is linear in the belief (Lemma~\ref{lem:range}(ii)), so $\alpha_\sigma \cdot b_{k+1}(h) = \sum_{m'} \Pr_{k+1}(m' \mid h)\, V_{m'}(h, \sigma)$. The receding plan of level $k+1$ is a maximizer of $\alpha_\sigma \cdot b_{k+1}(h)$ over all conditional $H$-step plans, by backward induction in the finite POMDP of Lemma~\ref{lem:range}(ii): with $r$ steps to go it maximizes $Q^r$ at its own updated belief. This gives the inequality and the equality condition. For (i), the bracket for $m' \neq m$ is at most $u_H - \ell_H$ in magnitude, since both values lie in $[\ell_H, u_H]$, so $(1 - \epsilon)$ times the bracket for $m$ is at least $-\epsilon (u_H - \ell_H)$. (ii) is a rearrangement of the inequality. For (iii), $i$'s own actions are known to $i$, so the posterior $\Pr_{k+1}(m \mid h)$ does not depend on the policy that chose them, and the joint law of $m$ and $h$ under the prior is $\mu(m) \Pr_m(h)$; summing the inequality over histories weighted by $\Pr(h)$ gives (iii).
\end{proof}

The theorem says nothing about an opponent outside the support ($m > k$), and nothing about whole receding-horizon episodes, whose later decisions are taken at histories that the two levels reach with different probabilities. Both gaps are real: the experiments below find losses in each. It also holds against every plan, not specifically the plan of one level less. The statement specific to depth concerns the level that only the deeper agent models.

\begin{theorem}[Dominance at the boundary level]\label{thm:boundary}
Assume (A1)--(A5), exact agents at levels $k$ and $k+1$ whose models of each lower level are the real agents of
that level, and \emph{nested priors}: $\mu_{k+1}(m')=(1-\rho)\,\mu_k(m')$ for $m'<k$ and $\mu_{k+1}(k)=\rho>0$
(uniform priors satisfy this with $\rho=1/(k+1)$). Let the true level be $m=k$, and let $h$ be a history of $i$
with $\Pr_k(h)>0$ and $\Pr(h\mid m=k)>0$. Write $b_l(h)$ for the belief of level $l$ at $h$,
$b^k(h)$ for the exact conditional belief given level $k$, and $\lambda_h=\Pr_{k+1}(m=k\mid h)\in(0,1]$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixture}
b_{k+1}(h)=(1-\lambda_h)\,b_k(h)+\lambda_h\,b^k(h),
\end{equation}
and
\[
V_k(h,\sigma_{k+1})-V_k(h,\sigma_k)\;\ge\;\frac{1-\lambda_h}{\lambda_h}\,
\big[\alpha_{\sigma_k}\cdot b_k(h)-\alpha_{\sigma_{k+1}}\cdot b_k(h)\big]\;\ge\;0 .
\]
The plan gain against the level that only the deeper agent models is nonnegative at every such history, without
weighting, and it is at least the deeper plan's regret under the shallower agent's belief times the posterior
odds against the true level.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $m'<k$ both agents hold the same model of level $m'$, so both assign $h$ the likelihood $\Pr(h\mid m')$
and the conditional belief $b^{m'}(h)$. By nesting, $\Pr_{k+1}(m'\mid h,m'<k)=\Pr_k(m'\mid h)$, which gives
(\ref{eq:mixture}). $\sigma_k$ maximizes $\alpha_\sigma\cdot b_k(h)$ and $\sigma_{k+1}$ maximizes
$\alpha_\sigma\cdot b_{k+1}(h)$ over conditional plans (backward induction, as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:dominance}). Write $D=\alpha_{\sigma_{k+1}}-\alpha_{\sigma_k}$. Optimality of $\sigma_{k+1}$
and (\ref{eq:mixture}) give $(1-\lambda_h)\,D\cdot b_k(h)+\lambda_h\,D\cdot b^k(h)\ge0$; optimality of
$\sigma_k$ gives $D\cdot b_k(h)\le0$. Hence $\lambda_h\,D\cdot b^k(h)\ge-(1-\lambda_h)\,D\cdot b_k(h)\ge0$, and
$D\cdot b^k(h)=V_k(h,\sigma_{k+1})-V_k(h,\sigma_k)$. Mixtures over tied maximizers preserve both inequalities.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
(i) $\Pr_k(h)>0$ holds whenever level $k$'s prior has level-0 mass and $h$ is possible, because the
no-information model gives every action positive probability; this is the role of level 0 in every prior.
(ii) When $m<k$ the truth is a component of both beliefs and no pair of optimality conditions isolates it:
only the weighted inequality of Theorem~\ref{thm:dominance} remains, and losses occur. (iii) The argument is
not specific to levels: an exact agent whose prior adds one hypothesis to another exact agent's prior, in
proportion, never loses to it at a decision when the added hypothesis is true.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Receding-horizon decomposition]\label{prop:telescope}
Fix the true opponent, let $\pi_l$ be the receding policy of level $l$ (at $h$ it commits to the plan
$\sigma_l(h)$ and executes its first action), $\mathbb E_l$ the expectation over $i$'s histories under $\pi_l$,
$J_T(\pi_l)=\mathbb E_l\sum_{t<T}\gamma^tr_t$, $g_l(h)=V(h,\sigma_l(h))$ the true $H$-step value of the plan
committed at $h$, and $V^{H-1}(h',\sigma|_o)$ the true value of the remaining $H-1$ steps of a plan after
observation $o$. Define the \emph{replanning drift} and the \emph{cross drift}
\[
\zeta_l(h_t)=\gamma\,\mathbb E_l\big[g_l(h_{t+1})-V^{H-1}(h_{t+1},\sigma_l(h_t)|_{o_{t+1}})\,\big|\,h_t\big],
\quad
\xi(h_{t+1})=g_k(h_{t+1})-V^{H-1}(h_{t+1},\sigma_{k+1}(h_t)|_{o_{t+1}}).
\]
Then, with $G(h)=g_{k+1}(h)-g_k(h)$ the plan gain at $h$,
\begin{align*}
J_T(\pi_l)&=g_l(h_0)+\sum_{t<T}\gamma^t\,\mathbb E_l[\zeta_l(h_t)]-\gamma^T\,\mathbb E_l[g_l(h_T)],\\
J_T(\pi_{k+1})-J_T(\pi_k)&=\sum_{t<T}\gamma^t\,\mathbb E_{k+1}[G(h_t)]
+\sum_{t<T}\gamma^{t+1}\,\mathbb E_{k+1}[\xi(h_{t+1})]-\sum_{t<T}\gamma^t\,\mathbb E_k[\zeta_k(h_t)]
-\gamma^T\big(\mathbb E_{k+1}-\mathbb E_k\big)[g_k(h_T)].
\end{align*}
The cumulative gain and the plan gains along the deeper agent's histories therefore differ by the three drift
terms, and can have opposite signs whenever these outweigh the plan gains.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For any function $\Phi$ of histories,
$\sum_{t<T}\gamma^tr_t=\Phi(h_0)-\gamma^T\Phi(h_T)+\sum_{t<T}\gamma^t[r_t+\gamma\Phi(h_{t+1})-\Phi(h_t)]$.
With $\Phi=g_l$ under $\pi_l$: $g_l(h_t)=\mathbb E[r_t\mid h_t]+\gamma\,\mathbb
E[V^{H-1}(h_{t+1},\sigma_l(h_t)|_{o_{t+1}})\mid h_t]$, since the executed action is the plan's first, so the
bracket has conditional mean $\zeta_l(h_t)$. With $\Phi=g_k$ under $\pi_{k+1}$:
$\mathbb E[r_t+\gamma g_k(h_{t+1})\mid h_t]=g_{k+1}(h_t)+\gamma\,\mathbb E[\xi(h_{t+1})\mid h_t]$, so the
bracket has conditional mean $G(h_t)+\gamma\,\mathbb E[\xi(h_{t+1})\mid h_t]$. Subtract the first identity for
$l=k$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The exact check.} For every cell of the level grids (tiger, coordination and published, $k \to k+1$ for $k \leq 3$; UAV and the Wumpus world, both roles, $k \leq 1$), we enumerate $i$'s histories against the true opponent, as played by the exact agent at level $k$ and, separately, at level $k+1$, and at each history we evaluate both levels' receding plans exactly against the true opponent. Comparing at the same history removes the timing effect of receding-horizon play. Each cell runs until its step count or a cap of 4~GB (8~GB for the four largest tiger cells) and 1~CPU-hour; 107 of the 116 cell-references stop at the memory cap, which fixes the number of steps covered.

Over the 116 cell-references, 601{,}820 histories are scored. The inequality of Theorem~\ref{thm:dominance} holds at every one: the smallest residual is 0 and the mixture of the plan values differs from level $k+1$'s maximum by at most $2.1 \times 10^{-11}$. Where a cell overlaps the exact pair curves of Section~7 of the paper, the cumulative reward of the reference play reproduces them to $7.9 \times 10^{-12}$ at 545 steps. Re-enumerating every class (b) cell with the posterior odds and the regret of the deeper plan under the shallower agent's belief, the stronger inequality of the theorem holds at each of the 8{,}628 histories (smallest margin 0; nontrivial, with positive regret and $\lambda_h < 1$, at 3{,}154), and the mixture identity holds to $2.6 \times 10^{-15}$. \section{Near-Tie Escalation}
\label{sec:escalation}

Compression with a small bound $N$ can change a decision only where the decision is close, and the planner measures closeness itself, by the gap between its two largest scored values. Each agent at level 2 or higher keeps a \emph{shadow}: the same planner, with the same seeds, at a larger bound $N' > N$, conditioned on the same realized actions and observations. When the base planner's top-two gap is at most a threshold $g$, the shadow decides; otherwise the base decision stands. Modeled agents are solved by the base planner only. We use $N = 64$, $N' = 1{,}024$ at level 2 and 256 at levels 3--4, and $g = 3$ in reward units. On 1{,}600 further coordination episodes, escalation removed 32 of the 34 compression near-ties of Section~7.2 of the paper and added none, escalating 3.2\% of the decisions at 0.41 times the CPU of the larger bound throughout.

\paragraph{Multi-agent tiger.} Two agents face two doors, one hiding a tiger and the other a prize. Each agent can listen or open a door; listening costs 1, opening the tiger's door costs 100 and opening the other door pays 10, and if either agent opens a door the tiger is placed at random. In the \emph{coordination} game, an agent that opens the safe door in the same step as the other agent receives 40 instead of 10.

\paragraph{UAV reconnaissance.} An unmanned aerial vehicle $i$ seeks a ground target $j$ on a $3 \times 3$ grid of sectors. Both agents move to an adjacent sector or stay. The UAV knows its own sector and, when it stays, observes the target's row with probability 0.8; the target knows its own sector and observes the UAV's row with probability 0.6. \paragraph{Two-agent Wumpus world.} A human $i$ searches a $3 \times 3$ grid with one pit for gold, while a wumpus $j$ hunts the human. The human can move forward, turn, grab and shoot one arrow; the wumpus can move forward and turn. Both agents perceive deterministic sets of tokens: the human perceives bump, stench, breeze, glitter and scream, and the wumpus perceives bump, breeze, glitter and the human's footsteps within distance 2. The human receives 1000 for the gold and $-1000$ for dying, and the wumpus 1000 for catching the human and $-1000$ for being shot; every other step costs 1.

\section{The Released Variant of I-NTMCP}

The released code of I-NTMCP refills a belief after a real step by rejection sampling and, after 3{,}000 rejections, fills the rest with particles whose own observation contradicts the real one. Under the Wumpus world's deterministic percepts this \emph{force-filling} grows with the budget: in the 60 level-2, horizon-2 problems of Table~1 of the paper, the median force-filled share is 0, 0.17 and 0.43 at $M = 1{,}000$, 4{,}000 and 16{,}000 (maximum 0.99), and the released variant is optimal on 19, 32 and 29 of 60 problems, against 17, 32 and 34 for the reinvigorated variant, which is our own modification and not part of the authors' code. In the 20-step UAV episodes at horizon 4, the released variant force-fills 31--49\% of its beliefs on average at $M \geq 4{,}000$; it earns 17.5, 20.8, 32.2 and 35.0 at $M = 1{,}000$ to 64{,}000 ($n = 30$), and is optimal on 73--88\% of the exactly scored decisions for $M \leq 16{,}000$, against 120 of 120 for I-POMCP at budget 1{,}000.

\section{Fidelity of the Baselines}

\paragraph{I-NTMCP.} On the authors' own Runner-Chaser $4 \times 4$ problem (Schwartz et al., 2022), their runner, unchanged, reproduces their published results exactly: the runner earns 65.964 (standard deviation 71.2) against a random chaser and 95.998 against a level-0 chaser, over 1{,}000 episodes each. We restated the problem as finite transition, observation and reward functions, which agree with the authors' model on every state and joint action, and ran the same planner (nesting level 1, 1{,}024 simulations, the published exploration constant) through our adapter: it earns 65.57 (71.6) against the random chaser, a difference of $-0.40$ within the two-sample 95\% half-width of 6.26, and reaches safety in 847 of 1{,}000 episodes against the authors' 849. The authors' code also reproduces their published $3 \times 3$ and $7 \times 7$ results exactly. Through our adapter, the mean return of their runner lies within the two-sample 95\% interval of the published value in all six match-ups of their comparison ($3 \times 3$, $4 \times 4$ and $7 \times 7$, against a random chaser and a nesting-level-0 chaser; $n = 1{,}000$ each): 99.0 against 99.0 twice, 65.57 against 65.96, 96.00 against 96.00, 83.85 against 85.05, and 86.45 against 87.63. A search through the adapter costs 1.45 times the CPU of the authors' runner, so the CPU comparisons of the paper charge I-NTMCP up to that factor.

\paragraph{IPF + RTS.}\label{sec:fidelity-ipf} We implement the interactive particle filter and reachability-tree sampling of Doshi and Gmytrasiewicz (2009) line by line from their Figs.~4, 5 and 18. The other agent's level-0 belief is updated exactly inside each particle; the other agent's observations are enumerated and weighted rather than sampled; $N$ particles are resampled with replacement after weighting; the lookahead backs up the distinct sampled observations, each weighted by its exact likelihood under the particle belief; and level-0 models are solved exactly. On their multiagent tiger (their Table~1, which equals our domain), with their prior, protocol and discount (10 policy trees, 100 runs each), the filter agrees with their Fig.~15(a) only in part. We fixed in advance a bar of 90\% of a panel's points within the joint 95\% error, and no panel meets it. With $j$ acting as its exact model, as described, 5 of 7 points match at horizon 2, where the filter also reaches their exact value $-2.0$ at $N \geq 500$, and 1 of 7 match at horizon 3. Their exact line at horizon 3 is not reproduced either: it is 2.7, and ours is 1.01. Their line equals the single-agent optimum (2.72), which an I-POMDP agent can attain only if $j$ never resets the tiger, so after this mismatch we added a diagnostic arm in which the simulated $j$ always listens. That arm matches 4 of 7 points at horizon 2 and 6 of 7 at horizon 3, although its exact value at horizon 3, 2.21, is still below their line. That their simulations used such a $j$ is our inference and is not stated in their paper. Their Table~3 errors at horizon 3 (the exact value minus the worst tree: 4.39 at $N = 100$ and 2.76 at $N = 1{,}000$) are not matched: ours are 4.89 and 3.13 as described, and 3.89 at both $N$ when $j$ always listens. With observation sampling (their Fig.~17), our implementation agrees with the published curve at $K \geq 10$ but earns far more at small $K$ ($-1.3$ against $-20.5$ at $K = 2$, $N_p = 50$). Executing one tree per episode and following a sampled branch when the real observation was not sampled reproduces the published losses at $K \leq 3$ but not the recovery at larger $K$. So we follow the paper's description, which replans at every step; this is the stronger of the two readings, and the comparisons in the paper use it. Where the text leaves a choice open, the published configuration contains readings of our own: an observation that was not sampled counts zero in the backup; a nested branch with no support is dropped; $j$'s prior is our 100-point grid; and in hierarchies deeper than theirs the level-1 models are solved exactly.

\section{A Wide Initial Belief}
\label{sec:wide}

In coordination tiger at level 2 with $H = 3$, let $i$'s prior be $b_i(s, \theta_j) = b(s)\, w(\theta_j)$ with $b(s) = 1/2$, where $\theta_j$ is one of $M$ level-1 models of $j$, each of weight $1/M$, whose belief that the tiger is behind the left door is $p_m = (m - \frac{1}{2})/M$. $M = 1$ is the point prior of the other experiments. We enumerate every history of $i$ of length at most 2 (343 histories, the same at every $M$); because opening a door resets the tiger, they reach 31 distinct beliefs at $M = 1$ and 39 at $M \geq 2$. Every decision is scored against the exact I-POMDP under the same prior (Table~\ref{tab:wide}). A planner that ignores the width, that is, the exact decision under $M = 1$ scored under the wide prior, is wrong on 8 of 39 beliefs at $M = 2$ and on 14 of 39 at every $M \geq 8$ (losses up to 11.3).

\section{Anytime UAV Episodes}

A level-2 UAV plans with $H = 4$ or 5 in 20-step episodes from the common prior against a level-1 I-POMCP target ($n = 30$ per planner and budget). The exact solver cannot play such episodes, since its cost per decision grows about threefold with each step of history, but it can score their first decisions. I-POMCP uses root budgets of 50 to 1{,}000 simulations and models the target with the same budget, and I-NTMCP uses $M$ from 1{,}000 to 64{,}000. The decisions at the first five steps of each $H = 4$ episode are scored exactly; the exact solver's median CPU on them grows from 10~s at the first step to 710~s at the fifth. I-POMCP at $H = 4$ earns 34.8 at 2.9 s per decision (35 is the most any episode earned), where I-NTMCP earns 20.8 at 3.7 s and 32.2 at 13 s. With no more CPU than I-NTMCP, I-POMCP earns 2.7 [1.7, 3.7] more than I-NTMCP at $M = 16{,}000$ and 2.5 [1.2, 4.0] more at $M = 16{,}000$ and $H = 5$ (Holm $p \leq 0.01$), and 14.0 [4.3, 27.5] more at $M = 4{,}000$ (Holm $p = 0.12$); at $M = 64{,}000$, 55 s per decision, both always spot the target after four steps. Over the first five decisions at $H = 4$, I-POMCP at budget 1{,}000 is optimal on 120 of 120 at a median of 15~s per decision, against 51~s for the exact solver on the same decisions, I-NTMCP on 73--88\% for $M \leq 16{,}000$, and IPF + RTS with the sparse backup ($K = 2$, over 1{,}100 s per decision, 10 episodes) on 62--70\%. IPF + RTS as published was not run in these episodes; on the 80 problems of Table~\ref{tab:decisions-supp} at $H = 4$ it is optimal on 45--58 at 31--36 s per decision. \section{Further Results}
\label{sec:further}

\paragraph{Episodes inside the exact window.} The exact pair's expected reward lies within the I-POMCP pair's 95\% interval at 97 of 100 agent-steps of the coordination tiger grid and at 31 of 36 of the UAV grid.

\paragraph{Beliefs outside the support.} A level-2 tiger agent comes to believe that the other agent is of level 1 whether it is of level 1, 2, 3 or 4 (0.92--0.95 after 10 steps). It represents only levels 0 and 1, and its posterior concentrates on the hypothesis whose predictions are closest to the truth in Kullback--Leibler divergence (Berk, 1966). A level-2 UAV rules out a level-1 target as soon as the target's predicted flight fails to happen, at step 5.

\paragraph{Baselines in the Wumpus world.} The particle filter of IPF + RTS fails on 35 of the 219 reachable beliefs (16\%), because every particle contradicts the agent's own deterministic percept; with likelihood smoothing it fails on 5 and is optimal on 47--48\%. I-NTMCP is optimal on 77\% at $M = 64{,}000$, with losses up to 639. In UAV reconnaissance, with the target's exact policy in place of its softmax prediction, I-NTMCP is optimal on 73--78\% at every $M$, so its errors come from predicting the target.

\paragraph{Priors.} In coordination tiger at level 3, a point prior one level too high loses 11.9 [9.1, 14.8] by step 20 against the correct prior ($n = 1{,}000$; exact $-1.98$ by step 3). At level 4 every prior gives exactly the same value inside the exact window.

\section{Reproducibility}

\paragraph{Computing infrastructure.} All experiments ran on one machine with an Intel Core Ultra 9 275HX (24 cores) and 23~GB of memory under Linux (WSL2), with Python 3.12. Every planner is single-threaded and runs one decision or episode per process; we report the process CPU time of a decision, nested solves included, so that times do not depend on how many processes share the machine. The CPU times of Figure~1 of the paper come from one re-run of every planner and the exact solver at seed 0 on an otherwise idle machine; every decision of I-POMCP and of the exact solver, and every baseline decision on the same problem, reproduced the original run exactly.

\paragraph{How budgets were chosen.} For I-POMCP, the root budget was tried over $\{100, 250, 1{,}000, 4{,}000, 16{,}000, 64{,}000\}$ simulations, the modeled budget over $\{100, 400, 1{,}600, 6{,}400\}$ and sequential stopping capped at 6{,}400, and the belief bound over $N \in \{32, 64, 256, 1{,}024\}$ and exact beliefs. On each grid of named beliefs (Table~\ref{tab:decisions-supp}) we chose the smallest budget at which every decision was optimal against the exact I-POMDP, and then tested it, unchanged, on beliefs and seeds that the selection did not use (960 random tiger beliefs; 219 reachable Wumpus beliefs). The near-tie escalation threshold $g = 3$ was fixed before its held-out test. No parameter of a baseline was tuned on our problems: IPF + RTS and I-NTMCP were run over ladders of their own budgets, and every arm is reported.

\paragraph{Final settings.} Unless stated, $\gamma = 0.95$ and the exploration constant is $c = 1$. Sequential stopping uses 200-simulation pilots, 4 trees and $\alpha = 0.01$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Tiger decisions: level 2, horizon 3, root 1{,}000, modeled 400, exact beliefs; level 3, horizon 3, root 1{,}000, modeled stopping capped at 6{,}400 for level 2 and 100 for level 1; horizon 2, root 100, modeled 100, $N = 32$.
\item Tiger level grid: horizon 2, 100 simulations at the root and in every modeled solve, $N = 64$. Near-tie escalation (separate episodes, Section~7.2): $N' = 1{,}024$ at level 2 and 256 at levels 3--4, $g = 3$.
\item UAV decisions: horizons 2--3, root 1{,}000, target 100 (horizon 2) or 400 (horizon 3), $N = 64$; horizon 4, root 16{,}000 with an exact or 6{,}400-simulation stopped target; level 1 at horizon 5, root 64{,}000. At reachable beliefs (Section~7.1), root 1{,}000, target 1{,}600, exact beliefs.
\item UAV level matrix: horizon 3, root 1{,}000, $N = 64$, the same budget for every modeled level.
\item Wumpus world: horizon 2, exact beliefs, root and modeled 400 (episodes and reachable beliefs) or root 1{,}000 and modeled 400 (decision grids).
\item IPF + RTS, as published (Section~\ref{sec:fidelity-ipf}): $N = 64$ particles at every level above level 0, level 0 exact, $K \in \{2, 4, 8\}$ sampled observations per node, smoothing $\epsilon = 0.01$ where stated. Our earlier variant (every level as particles, the other agent's observations sampled, the mean over the $K$ samples as backup, level 0 by RTS) appears only where marked ``sparse backup''.
\item I-NTMCP: the authors' defaults (exploration constant $R_{\max} - R_{\min}$, uniformly random rollouts, $1/16$ extra particles per step), $M \in \{1{,}000, 4{,}000, 16{,}000, 64{,}000\}$, discount horizon set so that the search depth equals $H - 1$; the reinvigorated variant (our modification) allows 50 rejections per particle.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Code.} The code archive submitted with this supplement contains the planner, the exact references, the baselines' adapters, the domains, the test suite and the scripts of every experiment in the paper; it will be released under the MIT license upon publication. I-NTMCP is the authors' public implementation, used unchanged.
